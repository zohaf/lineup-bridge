0 · Diagnosis

Why the current wordmark stopped working

The bold italic Times was drawn for a big, 52px, editorial header. Now the whole product speaks in the system sans at 600, so the logo is the only serif and the only italic on the page. At 20–28px its thin hairlines and slant make it look like it belongs to a different company. Two ways out, both used by established brands: (a) a wordmark that shares the UI's skeleton (Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Anthropic), or (b) a deliberate contrast on exactly one axis — style, width or spacing — with matched x-height, plus a small icon that does the recognisable work (Peec, Spotify, Apple).

1 · Wordmark typography

Five fonts that sit with the system font

The method, so the choice isn't a matter of taste alone: (1) match x-height to the UI font within ~10%, so the wordmark and the 14px nav links sit on the same visual line; (2) contrast on one axis only (classification, width or spacing), never two; (3) it must hold at 18–20px and in one color; (4) it must be free to license as a wordmark (all five are SIL Open Font License, so we can outline the letters and own the drawing). The x-height and cap-height ratios below are measured live in your browser against the system font you are seeing.

2 · Brand icon

Five marks from the DJ booth

Each one has to work at 16px as a favicon, on beige, black and orange, and say one thing. The established rule (Apple, Spotify, Airbnb): a mark is a simple filled shape with one idea, readable as a silhouette. Pick one with Use and it flows into the lockups below.

4 · Landing background

Speaker mesh under the beige, revealed by the cursor

Plain beige by default; move the mouse (or drag a finger) over the hero and a speaker grille appears around the pointer and fades away. It's one CSS mask plus ~10 lines of JS, no images and no library, so it costs almost nothing to build or load. The text and buttons stay above it and are never covered.

5 · References and recommendation

Where the patterns come from

Established patterns used here

  • Peec AI: small icon + name in a 60px bar, logo ~20px.
  • Apple: icon alone in the nav.
  • Anthropic: wordmark that gives way to the mark.
  • Linear, Vercel, Stripe: wordmark drawn from the same family as the UI.
  • Braun / Dieter Rams, Teenage Engineering: perforated grilles, knobs and mono labelling as brand language.
  • Resident Advisor, DICE, Boiler Room: wide or heavy grotesks in nightlife branding.

My recommendation to start from

Wordmark in Instrument Serif (keeps the serif you already own, loses the italic and the thin bold) or Inter Tight (most seamless). Icon: the fading dot or the knob. Header: pattern A at 20px, icon-only on the phone. Background: perforated, medium reveal, hero only.
